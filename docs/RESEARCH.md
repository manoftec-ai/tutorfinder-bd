# RESEARCH — Tutor Finder website for Bangladesh / Dhaka

> Date: 2026-09-21
> Scope: Is "find a good tutor in Dhaka/Bangladesh" a good website/business opportunity?
> Method: web research (news, market reports, competitor sites, user forum threads), verified 2026 data where cited.

## 1. THE PROBLEM IS REAL (demand side)

- Bangladesh has the **highest private-tutoring prevalence in Asia-Pacific**: 92% of
  Bangladeshi students received private tutoring (UNESCO/UNICEF report 2018).
- Addressable market: **~38–40 million active students** in Bangladesh. Best-performing
  platform (Caretutors, 13 years old) has served **less than 1%** of it.
- **1/3 of total household education spending in BD** goes to coaching centres and house
  tutors (BANBEIS 2014, cited by Daily Star 2025).
- CAMPE 2024: per-student household education spend rose **25% (primary) / 51% (secondary)**
  vs 2022 — driven mainly by coaching, private tutors, guidebooks.
- Admissions-coaching industry alone ≈ **Tk 14 billion/year** (TBS, Aug 2025).
- **Teacher shortage forces tutoring**: ~3,500 (≈20%) govt secondary posts vacant; Dhaka
  Collegiate School = **1 teacher per 51 students**; SSC pass rate hit a **16-year low**
  (68.45%) in maths/English — parents react by buying tuition.
- Dhaka premium: English-medium home tuition **Tk 8k–10k/month (Dhanmondi) to
  Tk 15k–18k (Gulshan)** (TBS, Dec 2025). One Dhaka family pays ~Tk 15k/month for an HSC
  child, of which ~Tk 12k is coaching+tutor (Dhaka Stream, Jul 2026). Admission-coaching
  courses Tk 18k–20k/student.
- Supply side: thousands of university students (esp. Dhaka) depend on tutoring income.
  Demand and supply both exist. What's broken is the middle.

## 2. THE TRUST GAP IS THE REAL OPPORTUNITY (intermediary side)

- The market is not broken on supply or demand — it is broken on **trust / infrastructure**.
  This is the near-unanimous finding of every deep analysis (Caretutors founder 4–5 months
  of interviews 2012; Daily Star 2020 investigation; TBS 2026).
- **"Tuition media" (informal agents + Facebook pages)** dominate and are predatory:
  - Take **40–70% of the tutor's first-month salary** (Caretutors 55% today is the
    *formal* high end; informal agents are worse per Daily Star: 50–70%).
  - Demand upfront registration/membership fees (Tk 500–1,000) and often deliver nothing
    (fake addresses, no refunds, documented since 2007).
  - Zero accountability: no verification, no dispute support, no protection for either side.
- **Guardians' problem**: letting a stranger into their home with a self-reported
  "BUET/DU" claim and no way to verify; quality is a lottery; no trial-standard process.
- **Tutors' problem**: no access without networks; sudden cancellations; withheld pay;
  "prefer a female tutor" discrimination; no record of their work.

**Key takeaways**
- Caretutors is a strong, profitable, 13-year leader with a real moat (verification ops, data,
  referral flywheel, brand). A newcomer **cannot out-general it** in the mass market.
- But the market is **not winner-take-all**: <1% penetration, informal channels still dominate,
  competitors are fragmented and mostly weak.
- Caretutors founder acknowledges trust is still their "massive issue", especially outside Dhaka.

## 4. MONETIZATION — PROVEN MODELS

- **Commission on placement (standard)**: tutor pays a % of first-month salary AFTER earning it
  (aligns incentives; removes upfront fee risk). Guardian pays nothing. Proven profitable.
- **Verification fee** (Tk 500) per confirmed hire (Caretutors).
- **Subscription** (Tuition Solution BD): unlimited applications for a period + referral bonus.
- **Premium/featured tutor listings** (directory model).
- Payments via **bKash / Nagad** are essential (any BD consumer play).

## 5. REGULATORY RISK — REAL BUT WEAK-ENFORCED

- Draft **Education Act 2026** proposes phasing out coaching centres/private tuition/guidebooks
  within 3–5 years via new rules. Draft was **criticised and pulled back** (Feb 2026); even if
  passed, existing 2012 policy fully banning teacher tutoring was **never enforced**.
- Likely scenario: demand shifts form (home/home-group/online) rather than disappearing.
- Implication: **don't build the whole business around institutional coaching centres**; home
  tutoring + online + special-skills categories are safer and are where demand is moving.

## 6. VERDICT

### The market: EXCELLENT (true structural gap, huge and cash-rich demand)
Crisis verified: teacher shortage + trustless middlemen + 92% tutoring prevalence + rising spend.

### The generic idea (another mass "Caretutors clone"): NO-GO
Too late to out-scale a 13-year dominant leader; distribution (campus/Facebook/offline) is brutal;
the trust flywheel takes years and needs an ops/verification team.

### The smart play: CONDITIONAL GO — niche wedge + SEO-first
A **new entrant wins via an underserved segment Caretutors serves thinly**, plus organic search:

1. **English-medium / foreign-curriculum** (Cambridge/IB/IGCSE) — highest fees, served mostly by
   word-of-mouth ("no official figures, no reliable data" per TBS Dec 2025).
2. **Female-tutor-only** requests (safety-conscious parents; female tutors prefer this filter) —
   also a brand/Care participation angle.
3. **Special / neurodiverse child education** (one of Caretutors' 13 categories, likely thin).
4. **Online tutoring & diaspora** (Bangladeshis abroad want Bangla/Arabic/Quran tutors) —
   arbitrary geography, no transport.
5. **SEO content + directory** ("home tutor Dhaka", "physics tutor Dhanmondi",
   "admission test tutor") pages per area/subject — capture search demand, funnel to a niche
   matching service. Zero budget (Astro + Vercel, existing infra).

Realistic expectation: 1–3 years of grind for meaningful revenue (Caretutors needed 13).
Not get-rich-quick; but a genuine, growing, proven-value market.
